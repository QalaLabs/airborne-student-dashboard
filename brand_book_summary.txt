LINE SPACING, TRACKING & USAGE GUIDELINES 04 — TYPOGRAPHY AIRBORNE AVIATION ACADEMY  |  BRAND BOOK 2026 Presented by Qala Labs LINE SPACING (LEADING) Body copy: 1.5× the font size. Headings: 1.1×. PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION 06 — IMAGERY STYLE AIRBORNE AVIATION ACADEMY  |  BRAND BOOK 2026 Presented by Qala Labs CORE RULE: Zero fake stock imagery. All photography must feature real students, real scores, and real environments. Authenticity is the competitive edge. PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION Capture real student progress — actual DGCA exam score sheets, classroom interaction, and alumni in airline uniforms. Every image should feel like it was taken at Airborne, not sourced from a photo bank. LIGHTING Bright, well-lit, and professional. Natural light preferred for classroom environments. No artificial moody or cinematic lighting rigs. The light should feel like clarity. MOOD Clarity · Focus · Structured Learning · Quiet Confidence. The image atmosphere should mirror the brand voice: authoritative without being intimidating, aspirational without being unrealistic. FRAMING Medium shots for classroom teaching interactions. Clear, focused portrait framing for success story thumbnails. Cockpit/airline imagery: wide establishing shots showing the aspirational destination.

=== SLIDE 25 ===
CROPPING RULES · FILTERS · IMAGE DO'S & DON'TS 06 — IMAGERY STYLE AIRBORNE AVIATION ACADEMY  |  BRAND BOOK 2026 Presented by Qala Labs CROPPING RULES Subject centered, sharp focus. Avoid abstract or extreme close-up crops that remove context. Portraits: head and shoulders minimum. Groups: full classroom frame. Never crop the AIRBORNE logo if present in-frame — maintain full lockup or remove entirely. COLOUR GRADING & FILTERS Natural colour temperature: 5500–6000K (daylight white balance). Slightly elevated clarity and sharpness — never soft or blurred. Brightness: +5–10 from natural. Contrast: +5 max. Zero moody grade, zero desaturation. Avoid: cinematic teal-orange, heavy vignettes, blur filters, and black-and-white except for archival contexts. ✓  DO Use real DGCA exam scores and student names. Showcase alumni in genuine airline uniforms. Bright, professional, naturally lit environments. Sharp focus, centred framing, clean backgrounds. Attribute every image: name, achievement, airline. ✕  DON'T Use generic stock pilot imagery. Use moody, dark, or cinematic filters. Pose students in unrealistic staged scenarios. Use heavily retouched or AI-generated people. Place branded images on cluttered backgrounds.

ILLUSTRATION STYLE & ICONOGRAPHY 07 — ILLUSTRATION AIRBORNE AVIATION ACADEMY  |  BRAND BOOK 2026 Presented by Qala Labs All illustration and iconography must feel engineered, precise, and aerodynamic — mirroring the AIRBORNE wordmark's cutting style. LINE Clean, sharp, uniform vector strokes. Consistent weight (1–2pt). Geometric, not organic. Used for aviation diagrams, subject icons, UI elements. Example: Compass arrows, flight path trajectories, DGCA subject icons FILLED Solid flat fills only — Aviation Blue or Ascend Red. Zero gradients. Zero drop shadows. Used for icon fills, infographic blocks, callout shapes. Example: Subject pictograms, stat callout icons, checklist markers HAND-DRAWN — PROHIBITED STRICTLY PROHIBITED. No sketches, brush strokes, or organic hand-rendered elements. All graphics must look calculated and engineered. Example: N/A — absolutely none permitted GEOMETRIC Highly geometric: arrows, compass points, wing silhouettes, upward trajectory indicators. Must incorporate angular, aerodynamic cuts echoing the logo's letterforms. Example: Navigation charts, subject matter diagrams, progress infographics ICONOGRAPHY: Custom subject icons for Air Navigation, Aviation Meteorology, Air Regulations, Technical General, RTR — all must use aerodynamic cuts mirroring the AIRBORNE wordmark. Source: Adobe Illustrator. Export: SVG + PNG@2×.
